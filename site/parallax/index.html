Layer 01 — Sky

The backdrop barely moves

The gradient sky is the slowest layer in the scene, drifting at just 2% of your scroll speed. Because it moves almost not at all, everything in front of it feels like it's racing forward — that's the whole trick of parallax: relative motion creates depth.

translateY = scrollY × 0.02
Layer 02 — Stars & Sun

Mid-distance objects drift gently

Stars twinkle at 5% speed and the low sun sinks at 15%. They're far away, so they lag behind your scroll — exactly how distant mountains seem to crawl past a car window while roadside fences whip by.

translateY = scrollY × 0.05 … 0.15
Layer 03 — Mountain ridges

Two ridges, two speeds

The far purple ridge slides at 30%, the nearer indigo ridge at 55%. Stacked SVG silhouettes with different factors sell the illusion that one range sits miles behind the other — no WebGL required.

translateY = scrollY × 0.30 / 0.55
Layer 04 — Mist & content

Foreground rushes past

Wisps of valley mist sweep by at 80–95% of scroll speed, nearly keeping up with you. Meanwhile these very cards fade and rise into view via IntersectionObserver — parallax for the scenery, reveals for the story.

translateY = scrollY × 0.80 … 0.95
